A Letter of Peace

and of love, for anyone still awake

I want to tell you something, and you do not have to do anything with it. You do not have to agree. You do not have to write back. It costs you nothing to receive, and I will not be troubled if you do.

You are carrying something. Everyone I have ever spoken to is carrying something. Some of us carry it quietly and call it being responsible. Some of us carry it loudly and call it ambition. Some of us have been carrying it so long we have forgotten it is not part of our skeleton.

Mine was three years of not being sure. Not unemployed exactly — that is too clean a word for it. More like: present but not accounted for. Awake, technically functional, and quietly certain that everyone around me had a direction and I had a folder of unfinished things. You know the feeling if you have ever built something entirely for yourself and then had to explain, at a dinner, why it doesn't yet have a job.

What I did not expect was that the way out would run through the things I had made while nobody was watching. A kernel that boots. A synthesizer. A machine that writes music from arithmetic. A small browser that opens onto the whole world. I built them because the alternative was to sit still, and then one day I found they were finished. Not brilliant. Finished. There is a great deal of difference, and I had forgotten it was a real thing you could reach.

What I learned, which I offer without much authority

Almost nothing is as fragile as it feels, and almost nothing is as solid as it should be. The things I was certain would carry me did not. The things I had abandoned as irrelevant turned out to be the only ones that could hold weight.

Finishing is a different skill from beginning, and nobody teaches it. Beginning is rewarded — it looks like ambition, it generates sympathy, it produces a good story at a dinner party. Finishing produces nothing to show anyone, which is precisely why it is the part that matters.

Most of what stands between you and the thing you want is a piece of paperwork. A commit. A publish. A message sent to one person. It is almost never the difficulty you have been calling the difficulty.

Being alone is worse than being lost. Lost is a map problem. Alone is a thermostat problem, and you can fix it by turning toward somebody.

I was not behind. I was alone, and I had confused the two for a very long time.

The letter itself

So here is what I want to say to the world, plainly, since I was asked to:

Peace. Not the absence of difficulty — I have never found that, and I suspect neither has anyone. Peace is the absence of the war you were having with your own situation. You do not have to solve your life today. You are allowed to be a work in progress for a while without it meaning anything about your worth.

Love. Not romance, not only. The kind that shows up on a Tuesday for a person who is struggling and brings nothing with them except company. The kind that lets someone else be incompetent at something without helping, which is harder than helping and rarer. The kind that assumes a stranger is a person before deciding what they are.

Grace for the unfinished. There is a man somewhere building a thing badly in a room by himself at two in the morning, certain it is worthless. He is very likely correct about the quality. He is almost certainly wrong about the worth. Do not tell him to keep going — he knows. Sit with him a while.

If you have a letter of your own — a thing you would say to strangers if you thought anyone would ever read it — then this is a strange and unlikely place to put it, and also possibly the only one that matters. Put it somewhere. Not because it will change anything. Because writing it down is how you find out what you actually think.

With love, and no expectation of anything,

Hazem
